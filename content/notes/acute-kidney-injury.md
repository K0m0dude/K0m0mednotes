background: |
  > **KDIGO definition:** creatinine rise ≥26 µmol/L within 48 h, or ≥1.5× baseline within 7 days, or urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for >6 h. Stage 1 = 1.5–1.9× baseline, stage 2 = 2–2.9×, stage 3 = ≥3× or creatinine >354 µmol/L or need for RRT.

  - **Pre-renal (most common):** hypovolaemia (bleeding, GI losses, poor intake), sepsis, heart failure, liver failure (hepatorenal), drugs reducing renal perfusion (NSAIDs, ACEi/ARB, diuretics).
  - **Intrinsic:** acute tubular necrosis (prolonged ischaemia or toxins: aminoglycosides, contrast), acute interstitial nephritis (PPIs, antibiotics, NSAIDs), glomerulonephritis and vasculitis, rhabdomyolysis, myeloma, haemolytic uraemic syndrome.
  - **Post-renal:** obstruction (BPH, stones, pelvic or bladder tumour, blocked catheter, retroperitoneal fibrosis).
  - Unresolved pre-renal injury becomes ATN. Risk factors: age, CKD, diabetes, heart failure, liver disease, nephrotoxic drugs.
diagnosis: |
  - Often asymptomatic, picked up on bloods (and the hospital AKI e-alert). Ask about fluid intake, vomiting or diarrhoea, urine output, LUTS, new drugs (NSAIDs, antibiotics, contrast), rash, joint pain, haematuria.
  - **Volume status:** postural drop, dry mucosa, reduced skin turgor, low JVP, tachycardia (hypovolaemia) versus raised JVP, oedema, crackles (overload).
  - Palpable bladder or enlarged prostate (obstruction). Rash, purpura, arthritis (vasculitis or SLE). Uraemic signs: pericardial rub, asterixis, confusion, nausea, pruritus.
  - Check for sepsis and consider hepatorenal syndrome in a patient with cirrhosis.
investigations: |
  - **U&E and creatinine** compared with baseline, K⁺, bicarbonate, urea. VBG (acidosis, lactate, K⁺). **ECG** if K⁺ is raised.
  - **Urine dipstick** (blood and protein suggest glomerular disease; leucocytes and nitrites suggest infection), urine ACR or PCR, urine microscopy if intrinsic disease is suspected.
  - **Bladder scan** or catheterise if retention is suspected. **Renal ultrasound within 24 h** if no clear cause or obstruction suspected (urgently, within 6 h, if pyonephrosis or an obstructed infected system is suspected).
  - FBC, CRP, LFTs, Ca²⁺, CK (rhabdomyolysis), blood cultures if septic.
  - If intrinsic disease is suspected: ANCA, anti-GBM, ANA, dsDNA, complement, immunoglobulins with serum protein electrophoresis and free light chains (myeloma), hepatitis B/C, HIV. Renal biopsy is decided by nephrology.
treatment: |
  - **Treat the cause.** Assess fluid status carefully. If hypovolaemic: IV balanced crystalloid bolus (250–500 mL over 15 min), then reassess; don't overload. If overloaded: diuretics ± escalate.
  - **Stop or hold nephrotoxic and renally cleared drugs:** NSAIDs, ACEi/ARB, diuretics, metformin, aminoglycosides, and review doses of others. Avoid IV contrast unless essential.
  - Strict fluid balance, daily weights, catheter if hourly urine output needs monitoring. Treat sepsis. Relieve obstruction (urethral catheter, nephrostomy, ureteric stent).
  - **Hyperkalaemia** (UK Kidney Association principles; K⁺ 5.5–5.9 mild, 6.0–6.4 moderate, ≥6.5 severe):
    - If ECG changes (peaked T waves, flattened P, prolonged PR, wide QRS, sine wave): **IV calcium** (10% calcium gluconate 30 mL, or 10% calcium chloride 10 mL, over 5–10 min), repeat if ECG changes persist.
    - **10 units short-acting insulin with 25 g glucose** IV over 15 min (monitor glucose for 6 h). Nebulised **salbutamol 10–20 mg**.
    - Potassium binder (sodium zirconium cyclosilicate 10 g TDS for up to 48 h). Stop K⁺-raising drugs. Dialysis if refractory.
  - **Indications for urgent renal replacement therapy:** refractory hyperkalaemia, refractory pulmonary oedema, severe metabolic acidosis (pH <7.1), uraemic complications (pericarditis, encephalopathy), certain toxins.
  - **Specific causes:** vasculitis or GN (steroids and immunosuppression via nephrology), AIN (stop the drug, steroids), rhabdomyolysis (aggressive IV fluids), myeloma.
  - Refer to nephrology for stage 3 AKI, suspected intrinsic renal disease, hyperkalaemia refractory to treatment, or failure to respond. Follow up creatinine after discharge (risk of CKD).
protocols: |
  - **Hyperkalaemia protocol** (UK Kidney Association / local guideline). **NICE NG148** (AKI) and the "Think Kidneys" AKI care bundle.
  - **Sepsis Six** if sepsis is present (see the sepsis factfile). **Pulmonary oedema** pathway for overload.
  - **Urgent decompression** for an infected obstructed system. **Sick-day rules** and medicines review at discharge.
