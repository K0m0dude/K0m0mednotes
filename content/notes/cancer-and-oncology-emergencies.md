background: |
  - Cancer arises from accumulated genetic and epigenetic changes leading to uncontrolled proliferation, evasion of apoptosis and invasion or metastasis. Common acute presentations relate to the primary, to metastases, to systemic effects (paraneoplastic), or to treatment.
  - **New presentation:** often non-specific. Unexplained weight loss, fatigue, night sweats, persistent pain, change in bowel habit, PR or PV bleeding, haemoptysis, dysphagia, iron-deficiency anaemia, lumps, new-onset venous thrombosis, or an emergency (obstruction, perforation, MSCC, SVCO, pleural effusion).
  - **Cancer of unknown primary (CUP):** metastases with no primary after standard workup. Managed by the CUP MDT.
  - **Paraneoplastic:** hypercalcaemia (PTHrP), SIADH (small-cell lung cancer), Lambert–Eaton, Cushing's (ectopic ACTH), hypercoagulability (Trousseau's), dermatomyositis, acanthosis nigricans.
  - **Treatment types:** surgery, radiotherapy, cytotoxic chemotherapy, targeted therapy, hormone therapy, **immunotherapy** (immune checkpoint inhibitors, CAR-T).
diagnosis: |
  - Follow NICE NG12 suspected-cancer referral criteria (urgent suspected cancer pathway, 28-day faster diagnosis standard). Ask about family history, smoking, occupational exposures, weight loss, and performance status (**ECOG 0–4**).
  - Examine for lymphadenopathy, masses, hepatomegaly, ascites, bone tenderness, skin lesions, pleural effusion, and neurology.
  - **Neutropenic sepsis:** temperature >38 °C (or other signs of sepsis) with neutrophils ≤0.5 × 10⁹/L (or expected to fall) in a patient on chemotherapy. May be hypothermic or afebrile if very unwell.
  - **Metastatic spinal cord compression (MSCC):** new back pain (thoracic, nocturnal, worse lying flat, or radicular), then limb weakness, sensory level, sphincter dysfunction. Any patient with cancer and new back pain needs assessment.
  - **Superior vena cava obstruction (SVCO):** facial and neck swelling, dyspnoea, engorged neck veins, plethora, Pemberton's sign; often lung cancer or lymphoma.
  - **Hypercalcaemia of malignancy:** thirst, polyuria, constipation, nausea, abdominal pain, confusion, short QT; corrected calcium >2.6 mmol/L (severe >3.5).
  - **Tumour lysis syndrome:** after chemotherapy in high-turnover tumours (leukaemia, lymphoma). ↑K⁺, ↑phosphate, ↓Ca²⁺, ↑urate, AKI, arrhythmia.
  - **Brain metastases:** headache, seizures, focal deficit, confusion. **Malignant bowel obstruction, malignant pleural or pericardial effusion** (tamponade), VTE and hyponatraemia (SIADH) are other frequent acute problems.
  - **Treatment-related toxicity:** chemotherapy (neutropenia, anaemia, thrombocytopenia, mucositis, diarrhoea, nausea, cardiotoxicity from anthracyclines and trastuzumab, nephrotoxicity from cisplatin, pulmonary toxicity from bleomycin, neuropathy from vincristine or oxaliplatin, extravasation); radiotherapy (mucositis, pneumonitis, skin, fatigue); hormonal therapy.
  - **Immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) toxicity** (pembrolizumab, nivolumab, ipilimumab, atezolizumab, durvalumab): immune-related adverse events (irAEs) can affect any organ, from weeks to months (and after stopping) treatment.
    - **Colitis:** diarrhoea, abdominal pain, blood or mucus (may perforate). **Pneumonitis:** cough, dyspnoea, hypoxia, ground-glass change on CT. **Hepatitis:** raised ALT, often asymptomatic.
    - **Endocrine:** hypothyroidism or thyrotoxicosis, **hypophysitis** (headache, fatigue, low cortisol), adrenal insufficiency, new T1DM or DKA. **Dermatitis, nephritis, myocarditis** (troponin, arrhythmia; high mortality), neurological (myasthenia, Guillain–Barré, encephalitis), arthritis.
investigations: |
  - General: FBC, U&E, LFTs, bone profile (corrected Ca²⁺), LDH, CRP, clotting, bone and lipid profile as relevant. **Tumour markers** only when guided (PSA, CA-125, AFP, β-hCG, CEA, myeloma screen); they are not screening tests.
  - CT chest, abdomen and pelvis for staging. **Biopsy for histology is essential** before treatment. PET-CT and MRI as directed. Discuss at the MDT.
  - **Neutropenic sepsis:** blood cultures (peripheral and from every line lumen), lactate, urine and sputum culture, CXR, FBC with differential, CRP, U&E, LFTs.
  - **MSCC:** urgent MRI whole spine (within 24 h if neurological symptoms or signs, within 1 week if pain suggests spinal metastases without neurology).
  - **Hypercalcaemia:** corrected Ca²⁺, PTH (suppressed in malignancy), PTHrP, vitamin D, U&E, ECG.
  - **Suspected irAE:** exclude infection first (stool culture, *C. difficile*, CT, cultures), then organ-specific tests (TFTs, cortisol and ACTH, glucose, troponin and ECG, LFTs, CK, CT chest, endoscopy or biopsy) and **early acute oncology advice**.
treatment: |
  - **New presentation:** stabilise, symptom control, diagnostic pathway, MDT, and discuss prognosis and goals honestly. Involve palliative care early for symptoms, advance care planning and DNACPR or ReSPECT discussions.
  - **Neutropenic sepsis:** treat as an emergency. **Broad-spectrum IV antibiotics (piperacillin-tazobactam unless allergic) within 1 hour** per local policy, plus resuscitation. Don't wait for the neutrophil count if a patient on chemotherapy is unwell. MASCC score helps risk-stratify. G-CSF at oncology's discretion.
  - **MSCC:** **dexamethasone 16 mg** (with gastric protection) as soon as suspected in patients with neurological symptoms, unless lymphoma is suspected (discuss with oncology or haematology first). Contact the acute oncology or MSCC coordinator immediately. Flat bed rest and spinal precautions until stability is assessed. Definitive treatment: radiotherapy or surgery.
  - **SVCO:** sit upright, oxygen, dexamethasone if lymphoma is likely, urgent CT, oncology for stenting, radiotherapy or chemotherapy.
  - **Hypercalcaemia:** IV 0.9% NaCl (3–4 L over 24 h if not overloaded), then **IV bisphosphonate** (zoledronic acid 4 mg or pamidronate) once rehydrated. Stop calcium and vitamin D and thiazides. Calcitonin for rapid effect. Denosumab if bisphosphonate contraindicated.
  - **Tumour lysis:** aggressive IV hydration, rasburicase (or allopurinol), K⁺ and Ca²⁺ management, renal replacement if required. Call haematology or oncology and critical care.
  - **Brain metastases:** dexamethasone (8–16 mg) with PPI, anti-seizure drugs if seizures, neurosurgery or oncology.
  - **ICI toxicity:** grade the toxicity, **hold the ICI**, contact the acute oncology service. Grade 2: prednisolone 0.5–1 mg/kg. Grade 3–4: admission, IV methylprednisolone 1–2 mg/kg. Refractory colitis: infliximab or vedolizumab. Refractory hepatitis or pneumonitis: mycophenolate or infliximab under specialist care. **Endocrinopathies**: replace hormones (replace cortisol before thyroxine). ICI myocarditis: high-dose steroids and cardiology.
  - **Symptom management:** WHO analgesic ladder, antiemetics matched to cause, laxatives with opioids, PPI with steroids, nutrition and dietitian input.
protocols: |
  - **Acute oncology service (AOS)**: 24/7 advice. Patients on systemic anticancer therapy carry an alert card and a 24-hour helpline number. Use the **UKONS triage tool**.
  - **NICE CG151** neutropenic sepsis: antibiotics within 1 hour of arrival. **NICE NG234** spinal metastases and MSCC. **NICE NG12** suspected cancer referral.
  - Any patient within about 12 months of ICI who becomes unwell (diarrhoea, breathlessness, headache, hypotension, hyperglycaemia) needs an irAE in the differential.
  - **Sepsis Six**, ABCDE, and VTE prophylaxis (high risk). End of life care: NICE NG31, ReSPECT and DNACPR.
