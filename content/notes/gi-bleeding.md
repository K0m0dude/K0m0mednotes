background: |
  - **Upper GI bleed (proximal to the ligament of Treitz):** peptic ulcer (H. pylori, NSAIDs, aspirin), **oesophageal or gastric varices**, Mallory–Weiss tear, erosive oesophagitis or gastritis, malignancy, angiodysplasia, Dieulafoy lesion.
  - **Lower GI bleed:** diverticular disease, angiodysplasia, haemorrhoids, colitis (inflammatory, ischaemic, infective), colorectal cancer or polyps, post-polypectomy. A brisk upper bleed can present as fresh PR blood.
  - **Risk factors:** NSAIDs, aspirin, anticoagulants and antiplatelets, SSRIs, alcohol, liver disease, previous bleeding, H. pylori, critical illness.
  - Severe bleeding leads to hypovolaemia, tissue hypoperfusion and organ failure. Older patients tolerate bleeding poorly and may not mount tachycardia (β-blockers).
diagnosis: |
  - **Haematemesis, coffee-ground vomit, melaena** (black, tarry, foul-smelling), haematochezia. Ask about NSAIDs, anticoagulants, alcohol, liver disease, previous ulcer, vomiting before haematemesis (Mallory–Weiss), weight loss and dysphagia (cancer).
  - **Shock:** tachycardia, hypotension, postural drop, pallor, cool peripheries, low urine output, confusion. Examine for signs of chronic liver disease, epigastric tenderness, and always do a **digital rectal examination**.
  - **Clues:** raised urea with normal creatinine suggests an upper source. Haemoglobin may be normal early because of haemoconcentration.
investigations: |
  - **Resuscitate first.** FBC (repeat), U&E (urea:creatinine), LFTs, **clotting and INR**, **group and save or crossmatch**, VBG with lactate, glucose, ECG and troponin if indicated.
  - **Glasgow-Blatchford score (GBS)** (before endoscopy): a score of 0–1 identifies patients who can be managed as outpatients. **Rockall** (after endoscopy) predicts rebleeding and mortality.
  - **Endoscopy (OGD)** within 24 h of admission after resuscitation, and **within 12 h** (or as soon as safely possible) if suspected variceal bleeding or unstable. Take biopsies or test for H. pylori.
  - Lower GI: colonoscopy when stable. **CT angiography** for ongoing bleeding with instability, then interventional radiology (embolisation).
treatment: |
  - **ABCDE.** Two large-bore IV cannulae, IV crystalloid to restore perfusion, oxygen if hypoxic, catheter and fluid balance, nil by mouth, high-dependency care if unstable.
  - **Transfusion (restrictive strategy):** transfuse if Hb <70 g/L, aiming for 70–90 g/L (higher threshold, about 80 g/L, if cardiovascular disease). In exsanguinating haemorrhage, **activate the major haemorrhage protocol** (red cells, plasma, platelets in balanced ratios). **Tranexamic acid is not recommended** for GI bleeding (HALT-IT trial: no benefit, more VTE).
  - **Anticoagulants and antiplatelets:** withhold. Warfarin: vitamin K plus PCC if life-threatening. DOACs: reversal agents if life-threatening bleeding. Restart aspirin (secondary prevention) once haemostasis achieved, as advised by the team.
  - **Non-variceal upper GI bleeding:** endoscopic therapy (adrenaline injection combined with thermal coagulation or clips) for high-risk stigmata. Then **high-dose PPI infusion** (80 mg bolus then 8 mg/h for 72 h) after endoscopic therapy. PPI before endoscopy is not recommended by NICE. Eradicate *H. pylori*. Stop NSAIDs. Surgery or IR if endoscopic haemostasis fails.
  - **Variceal bleeding:** **terlipressin 2 mg IV** (then 1–2 mg 4–6 hourly, for up to 5 days) started as soon as suspected, **prophylactic antibiotics** (e.g. ceftriaxone 1 g daily, for up to 7 days), **endoscopic band ligation**, restrictive transfusion. Rescue: balloon tamponade (Sengstaken–Blakemore tube) and early TIPSS for uncontrolled or early rebleeding. Then non-selective β-blocker (carvedilol or propranolol) and repeat banding.
  - **Lower GI bleeding:** most stop spontaneously. Investigate; treat the cause (endoscopic clip, embolisation, surgery). VTE prophylaxis with mechanical devices initially.
protocols: |
  - **Major haemorrhage protocol** (call 2222 or blood bank; trauma and obstetrics have separate ones). **Local upper GI bleeding pathway** (NICE CG141 / BSG guidance). **Variceal bleeding care bundle.**
  - **ALS** for arrest. **Escalate** if SBP <100, HR >100, ongoing haematemesis, rising lactate or transfusion requirement, and inform the endoscopist and on-call surgeon and IR early.
  - Patients in alcohol withdrawal also need thiamine and monitoring (see the drugs factfile). **Sepsis** can accompany variceal bleeding and cirrhosis.
