background: >+
  - Clinical syndrome of breathlessness, fatigue and fluid retention caused by
  structural or functional cardiac abnormality, with raised natriuretic peptides
  or objective evidence of congestion.

  - **By ejection fraction:** **HFrEF** (LVEF ≤40%), **HFmrEF** (41–49%),
  **HFpEF** (≥50%).

  - **Causes:** ischaemic heart disease, hypertension, valve disease,
  cardiomyopathy (dilated, alcohol, chemotherapy, viral), arrhythmia (AF,
  tachycardia), congenital, cor pulmonale, high-output states (anaemia,
  thyrotoxicosis), pericardial disease.

  - **Pathophysiology:** reduced cardiac output triggers neurohormonal
  activation (sympathetic nervous system, RAAS, vasopressin) leading to salt and
  water retention, vasoconstriction and adverse remodelling, which is the basis
  for treatment.

  - **Acute decompensation triggers:** missed medication, dietary salt or fluid
  excess, arrhythmia (AF), ischaemia or MI, infection, anaemia, renal failure,
  drugs (NSAIDs, negative inotropes, thiazolidinediones), uncontrolled
  hypertension, PE, valve failure.

diagnosis: >+
  - Symptoms: exertional dyspnoea, orthopnoea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea,
  ankle swelling, fatigue, weight gain, nocturnal cough.

  - Signs: **raised JVP**, displaced apex beat, third heart sound (gallop),
  bibasal crackles, pleural effusions, peripheral or sacral oedema,
  hepatomegaly, ascites. Cold, clammy peripheries with hypotension suggests
  **cardiogenic shock**.

  - **Acute pulmonary oedema:** severe breathlessness, frothy pink sputum,
  tachypnoea, hypoxia, widespread crackles.

  - Haemodynamic profiles: "warm and wet" (congested, well-perfused: most
  common) versus "cold and wet" (congested and hypoperfused: worse prognosis).

investigations: >+
  - **NT-proBNP** (NICE NG106: >2000 ng/L, urgent specialist assessment and echo
  within 2 weeks; 400–2000 ng/L, within 6 weeks). Levels are raised by age, AF,
  renal failure and lowered by obesity.

  - **ECG** (AF, LBBB, LVH, ischaemia), **CXR** (ABCDE: Alveolar oedema, Kerley
  B lines, Cardiomegaly, Dilated upper lobe vessels, pleural Effusions),
  **echocardiogram** (LVEF, valves, RV, pulmonary pressure).

  - Bloods: U&E (baseline for ACEi and diuretics), FBC, ferritin and TSAT (iron
  deficiency), TFTs, LFTs, HbA1c, lipids, troponin (ACS as a trigger), lactate
  in shock. ABG if respiratory distress.

  - Further: cardiac MRI, coronary angiography or CT coronary angiography, and
  ambulatory ECG as directed.

treatment: >+
  ### Acute heart failure


  - Sit upright, monitor, IV access, oxygen only if SpO₂ <94%. **IV loop
  diuretic:** furosemide 40–80 mg IV (higher if on regular diuretic, typically
  1–2.5× oral dose). Monitor urine output (± catheter), reassess response and
  repeat as needed.

  - **CPAP or NIV** for pulmonary oedema with respiratory distress or failure.
  IV nitrates for pulmonary oedema with high BP (avoid if SBP <100). Opioids are
  no longer recommended routinely.

  - **Cardiogenic shock:** urgent senior and critical care review, echo,
  inotropes or vasopressors, mechanical support, treat the cause (revascularise,
  arrhythmia).

  - Treat the trigger (ACS, AF, infection, anaemia). Daily weights, fluid
  balance, U&E and K⁺, and stop or hold harmful drugs (NSAIDs,
  non-dihydropyridine CCBs, thiazolidinediones).


  ### Chronic HFrEF: the four pillars


  - **ACE inhibitor** (or ARB, or ARNI sacubitril–valsartan), **β-blocker**
  (bisoprolol, carvedilol, nebivolol), **MRA** (spironolactone, eplerenone), and
  **SGLT2 inhibitor** (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin). Start low, go slow,
  monitor U&E and K⁺ after starting or changing doses.

  - Loop diuretics for symptom control. Add ivabradine (sinus rhythm, HR ≥75),
  hydralazine plus nitrate (particularly in patients of African or Caribbean
  family origin), digoxin, IV iron if iron deficient.

  - **Devices:** ICD (primary prevention where LVEF ≤35% despite therapy), CRT
  (LBBB with wide QRS). Cardiac rehabilitation, vaccinations, salt and fluid
  advice, self-monitoring of weight, specialist heart failure nurse follow-up
  within 2 weeks of discharge.

  - **HFpEF:** diuretics for congestion, SGLT2 inhibitor, treat hypertension, AF
  and obesity. Advanced HF: palliative care input.

protocols: >+
  - **Acute pulmonary oedema pathway** and **NICE NG106** (chronic HF) with ESC
  guidance. **ALS** for arrest, **ACS pathway** if ischaemic trigger.

  - **Escalate** if SBP <90, persisting hypoxia or acidosis on NIV, rising
  lactate, or falling urine output.

  - AKI is common when diuresing or starting ACEi (see the AKI factfile).
  **Avoid IV fluids** unless the patient is clearly hypovolaemic.
