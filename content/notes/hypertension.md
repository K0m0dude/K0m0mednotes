background: |
  - **Primary (essential) hypertension** (~90%): multifactorial (genetics, salt intake, obesity, age, alcohol, inactivity). **Secondary** (~10%): renal parenchymal and renovascular disease, **primary hyperaldosteronism** (Conn's), **phaeochromocytoma**, Cushing's, coarctation, thyroid disease, obstructive sleep apnoea, pregnancy (pre-eclampsia), drugs (NSAIDs, steroids, oral contraceptive, decongestants, ciclosporin, alcohol, cocaine).
  - Pathophysiology: raised peripheral resistance (RAAS, sympathetic overactivity, arterial stiffness) and sodium retention.
  - **Target organ damage:** LVH and heart failure, coronary disease, stroke, retinopathy, CKD, aortic dissection, peripheral arterial disease.
  - **Hypertensive emergency:** severe hypertension (often ≥180/120) with acute target organ damage: encephalopathy, intracerebral haemorrhage or ischaemic stroke, acute coronary syndrome, acute pulmonary oedema, aortic dissection, AKI, eclampsia, **papilloedema or retinal haemorrhages** (malignant or accelerated hypertension). **Hypertensive urgency**: severe hypertension without organ damage.
diagnosis: |
  > **NICE NG136 thresholds:** clinic BP ≥140/90 then confirm with ABPM (daytime average) or home BP (HBPM). **Stage 1:** clinic ≥140/90 and ABPM/HBPM ≥135/85. **Stage 2:** clinic ≥160/100 and ABPM/HBPM ≥150/95. **Stage 3 (severe):** clinic SBP ≥180 or DBP ≥120.

  - Usually asymptomatic. Emergency: headache, visual disturbance, confusion, seizures, chest pain, breathlessness, focal neurology.
  - Examination: BP in both arms, fundoscopy (hypertensive retinopathy, papilloedema), LVH, heart failure signs, radio-femoral delay (coarctation), renal bruits, abdominal masses (PKD), cushingoid features.
  - Look for causes in young patients (<40), resistant hypertension, severe or sudden onset, hypokalaemia, or unusual features.
  - **On the ward:** BP is often raised due to pain, anxiety, missed medication, fluid overload or acute illness. Repeat with the right cuff size, at rest, and treat the cause. Avoid aggressive BP lowering for asymptomatic raised readings.
investigations: |
  - **Assess target organ damage and cardiovascular risk:** urine ACR and dipstick (haematuria), U&E and eGFR, HbA1c, lipids, ECG, fundoscopy.
  - **Emergency:** ECG, troponin, CXR (oedema, widened mediastinum), CT head (neurology), CT aorta if dissection suspected, U&E, urinalysis, blood film, pregnancy test.
  - **Secondary causes:** renin and aldosterone (ARR), urinary or plasma metanephrines, renal artery imaging, cortisol tests, thyroid tests, sleep study.
treatment: |
  - **Hypertensive emergency:** admit to a monitored area (HDU or ICU). **IV labetalol** (or GTN for pulmonary oedema or ACS, nicardipine, sodium nitroprusside in critical care). Aim to reduce mean arterial pressure by **no more than about 25% in the first hour**, then to ~160/100 over 2–6 hours, and to normal over 24–48 h. Precipitous falls risk stroke and ischaemia.
  - **Specific exceptions:** **aortic dissection**: SBP <120 rapidly with IV β-blocker (labetalol, esmolol) ± vasodilator. **ICH:** lower SBP to about 130–140 (see stroke factfile). **Acute ischaemic stroke:** generally don't lower unless thrombolysing or >220/120. **Pre-eclampsia/eclampsia:** labetalol, nifedipine, hydralazine plus magnesium sulfate. **Phaeochromocytoma:** α-blockade first.
  - **Hypertensive urgency without organ damage:** gradual oral treatment over days, review adherence and cause.
  - **Chronic treatment (NICE NG136):** lifestyle (salt, weight, alcohol, exercise). **Step 1:** age <55 (or type 2 diabetes at any age) and not of Black African or Caribbean family origin: ACE inhibitor or ARB; age ≥55, or Black African or Caribbean family origin: calcium-channel blocker. **Step 2:** ACEi/ARB + CCB (ARB preferred to ACEi in people of Black African or Caribbean family origin), or ACEi/ARB + thiazide-like diuretic if a CCB is unsuitable. **Step 3:** ACEi/ARB + CCB + thiazide-like diuretic (indapamide). **Step 4 (resistant):** add spironolactone 25 mg (if K⁺ ≤4.5), or α- or β-blocker.
  - **Targets:** clinic <140/90 (age <80) or <150/90 (age ≥80); home or ABPM <135/85 (<145/85 if ≥80). **Statin** for high CV risk.
  - Monitor U&E after starting ACEi/ARB or diuretics.
protocols: |
  - **Hypertensive emergency management** (local guidance), **aortic dissection** pathway (CT aortogram, cardiothoracic and vascular referral), **stroke** BP thresholds, **pre-eclampsia** pathway with obstetrics.
  - Severe hypertension with headache, visual loss, chest pain or neurology means urgent senior review. **NICE NG136.**
