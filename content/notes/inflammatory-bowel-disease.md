background: |
  - Chronic relapsing immune-mediated inflammation of the gut in genetically susceptible individuals, triggered by environmental and microbiome factors.
  - **Ulcerative colitis (UC):** mucosal inflammation, always starting in the rectum and extending continuously proximally. Smoking is protective (non-smokers and ex-smokers are at higher risk).
  - **Crohn's disease:** transmural, patchy ("skip") inflammation anywhere from mouth to anus (commonly terminal ileum), with granulomas, strictures, fistulae, abscesses and perianal disease. **Smoking worsens Crohn's.**
  - **Extra-intestinal manifestations:** peripheral and axial arthritis, erythema nodosum, pyoderma gangrenosum, uveitis or episcleritis, **primary sclerosing cholangitis** (UC), gallstones and renal stones (Crohn's), osteoporosis, anaemia, and **increased VTE risk**.
  - Complications: toxic megacolon, perforation, massive haemorrhage, strictures and obstruction, fistulae, malnutrition, and colorectal cancer.
diagnosis: |
  - **UC:** bloody diarrhoea, urgency, tenesmus, nocturnal stools, crampy abdominal pain. **Crohn's:** abdominal pain (often RIF), non-bloody diarrhoea, weight loss, fever, mouth ulcers, perianal fistulae or abscess, obstructive symptoms.
  - **Acute severe UC (Truelove and Witts):** ≥6 bloody stools per day **plus** at least one of: HR >90, temperature >37.8 °C, Hb <105 g/L, ESR >30 mm/h (or CRP >30 mg/L).
  - Signs: pallor, tachycardia, fever, abdominal tenderness and distension, aphthous ulcers, clubbing, perianal disease, extra-intestinal signs. **Peritonism, absent bowel sounds and marked distension** suggest toxic megacolon or perforation.
  - Differentials: infective colitis (*C. difficile*, *Campylobacter*, *Salmonella*, CMV), ischaemic colitis, diverticular disease, colorectal cancer, coeliac disease, microscopic colitis, medication-induced (NSAIDs, checkpoint inhibitors).
investigations: |
  - **Stool:** culture and *C. difficile* toxin in every flare, faecal calprotectin (raised in active inflammation).
  - **Bloods:** FBC, CRP, U&E and K⁺, albumin, LFTs, ferritin, B12, folate, vitamin D, blood cultures if febrile.
  - **AXR:** colon >5.5–6 cm suggests toxic megacolon, mucosal islands and thumbprinting. **Erect CXR** or CT for perforation.
  - **Flexible sigmoidoscopy** (unprepared) with biopsies in severe UC. Colonoscopy with ileal intubation and biopsies when stable. Small bowel imaging (MR enterography, ultrasound, capsule) and MRI pelvis for perianal disease in Crohn's. Screen for HBV, HCV, HIV, TB (IGRA), VZV and CMV before immunosuppression.
treatment: |
  - **Acute severe UC (medical emergency):** admit, **IV hydrocortisone 100 mg QDS** (or methylprednisolone 60 mg daily), IV fluids, correct K⁺ and Mg²⁺, **LMWH VTE prophylaxis** (even with bleeding), nutritional support, stool chart, daily bloods and abdominal examination. Avoid **NSAIDs, opioids, anticholinergics and antidiarrhoeals (loperamide)** (risk of toxic megacolon). **Early joint gastroenterology and colorectal surgical review.**
  - **Day 3 review:** if stools >8/day, or CRP >45 mg/L with 3–8 stools/day (Oxford criteria), the risk of colectomy is high. Start **rescue therapy** (infliximab or ciclosporin, or other biologic or JAK inhibitor as per local policy) or plan surgery. **Urgent colectomy** for toxic megacolon, perforation, massive haemorrhage or failed medical therapy.
  - **Mild-moderate UC:** topical and oral 5-ASA (mesalazine), oral prednisolone 40 mg reducing course if inadequate response, budesonide MMX. **Maintenance:** mesalazine, thiopurines (azathioprine), biologics (infliximab, adalimumab, vedolizumab, ustekinumab, mirikizumab), and JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib, upadacitinib).
  - **Crohn's:** induction with steroids (prednisolone or budesonide), or exclusive enteral nutrition. Maintenance: azathioprine, methotrexate, biologics. Stop smoking. Surgery for strictures, fistulae, abscesses (with drainage and antibiotics), and failure of medical treatment.
  - Bone protection (calcium, vitamin D), vaccination, iron replacement (IV if severe), psychological support, and cancer surveillance colonoscopy for long-standing colitis.
protocols: |
  - **Acute severe colitis pathway** (BSG, ECCO, NICE NG130 for UC and NG129 for Crohn's). **C. difficile** testing and infection control (isolation, hand washing). **Sepsis Six** if septic.
  - **Red flags:** peritonism, fever with distension, HR >100 with rising CRP, colonic dilatation on AXR, or haemodynamic instability need same-day senior and surgical input.
  - Patients on immunomodulators or biologics can present atypically and have increased infection risk. Discuss with the IBD team.
