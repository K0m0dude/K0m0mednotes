background: >+
  - A seizure is a transient episode of abnormal, excessive or synchronous
  neuronal activity. Imbalance between excitation (glutamate) and inhibition
  (GABA).

  - **Provoked (acute symptomatic):** hypoglycaemia, hyponatraemia,
  hypocalcaemia, hypomagnesaemia, alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal, drugs
  (tramadol, TCAs, bupropion, cocaine), CNS infection, stroke, head injury,
  tumour, eclampsia, hypoxia.

  - **Unprovoked:** epilepsy (two unprovoked seizures more than 24 h apart, or
  one with a high recurrence risk). Sub-therapeutic anti-seizure medication or
  missed doses is a common trigger.

  - **Status epilepticus:** a seizure lasting ≥5 minutes, or two or more
  seizures without recovery of consciousness between them. Neuronal damage
  worsens with duration; it is a medical emergency.

diagnosis: >+
  - A **witness history** is the most important part. Ask about aura, onset
  (focal versus generalised), tongue biting (lateral), incontinence, duration,
  post-ictal state, and Todd's paresis.

  - **Generalised tonic-clonic:** loss of consciousness, tonic then clonic
  phases, post-ictal confusion for minutes to hours. **Focal:** preserved or
  impaired awareness with focal motor, sensory or automatism features.

  - **Convulsive syncope** is brief (seconds) with a clear trigger (standing,
  pain), pallor and rapid recovery. **Functional (dissociative) seizures** may
  show eye closure, side-to-side head movement, long duration and rapid
  recovery, without a raised prolactin or lactate.

  - Examine for focal neurology, meningism, head injury, signs of alcohol
  excess, and check pupils and glucose.

investigations: >+
  - **Capillary glucose immediately.** U&E, Na⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, LFTs, FBC, CRP, VBG
  (lactate is raised transiently after a tonic-clonic seizure), CK, alcohol
  level and toxicology, pregnancy test, anti-seizure drug levels where relevant.

  - **ECG** in every first seizure (long QT, arrhythmic syncope).

  - **CT head** if new focal deficit, head injury, anticoagulation,
  immunosuppression, persistent altered consciousness, or first seizure in
  adulthood with red flags. **LP** if meningitis or encephalitis suspected
  (after CT if indicated).

  - **EEG and MRI** are usually arranged by neurology, within 72 h if status or
  in hospital as indicated.

treatment: >+
  - **First aid:** note the time, protect from injury, nothing in the mouth,
  recovery position after convulsions stop, oxygen (high flow) and monitoring,
  check glucose, gain IV access.

  - **Status epilepticus (seizure ≥5 min):** first-line **IV lorazepam 4 mg**
  (0.1 mg/kg), repeat once after 10 min if still seizing. If no IV access:
  **buccal midazolam 10 mg** (or IM midazolam) or **rectal diazepam 10 mg**.

  - **Second line** (if seizing 20 min after starting first-line):
  **levetiracetam 60 mg/kg IV** (max 4.5 g) over 10 min, or **phenytoin 20 mg/kg
  IV** (max 2 g, ≤50 mg/min with ECG and BP monitoring), or **sodium valproate
  40 mg/kg IV** (max 3 g, avoid in women of childbearing potential).

  - **Refractory status:** ICU, general anaesthesia (propofol, midazolam or
  thiopentone) with EEG monitoring.

  - Correct causes: **10% glucose** for hypoglycaemia, **IV thiamine
  (Pabrinex)** in alcohol misuse, electrolyte correction, **magnesium sulfate 4
  g IV over 5–10 min then infusion for eclampsia**, antibiotics and aciclovir if
  CNS infection suspected.

  - **First seizure** that has resolved: no routine anti-seizure drug started in
  the ward. Refer to a first-seizure clinic (neurology within 2 weeks). Counsel
  on driving (DVLA: usually 6 months off after a first unprovoked seizure, 12
  months for epilepsy), bathing, heights and water.

protocols: >+
  - **Status epilepticus algorithm** (local and NICE guidance). **ABCDE** and
  NEWS2. Escalate to critical care early if not responding to second-line drugs.

  - **Eclampsia:** magnesium sulfate, obstetric emergency team.

  - Head injury, anticoagulation, or a fever or meningism with seizure: image
  and treat as CNS infection until proven otherwise (see the sepsis factfile).

  - Alcohol-related seizures: see the drugs factfile. Safeguarding and capacity
  considerations where relevant.
