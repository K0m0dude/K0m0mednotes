background: >+
  - Coronary artery disease (CAD) with atherosclerotic plaque narrows the
  epicardial vessels so that myocardial oxygen supply cannot meet demand on
  exertion or stress, causing **stable angina** (chronic coronary syndrome).
  Acute plaque rupture leads to ACS (see the ACS factfile).

  - Other mechanisms: coronary vasospasm (Prinzmetal), microvascular
  dysfunction, and severe aortic stenosis or anaemia (demand). Risk factors:
  age, male sex, smoking, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidaemia, family history,
  CKD, obesity, inactivity.

  - Consequences: ischaemic cardiomyopathy and heart failure, arrhythmia, and
  MI.

diagnosis: >+
  > **Typical angina requires all three:** (1) constricting discomfort in the
  chest, neck, jaw, shoulder or arm; (2) precipitated by physical exertion; (3)
  relieved by rest or GTN within about 5 minutes. **Atypical:** two of three.
  **Non-anginal:** one or none.


  - Examination is often normal. Look for risk factors (xanthelasma,
  hypertension, diabetes), anaemia, valve disease (aortic stenosis), heart
  failure, peripheral vascular disease.

  - **Red flags:** angina at rest, crescendo angina, new-onset within the last
  month, or prolonged pain mean **unstable angina/ACS**, which needs urgent
  assessment.

investigations: >+
  - Resting ECG, FBC, U&E, lipids, HbA1c, TFTs, and echo if murmur or suspected
  LV dysfunction.

  - **CT coronary angiography (CTCA)** is first-line for typical or atypical
  angina (NICE). If inconclusive: functional imaging (stress echo, myocardial
  perfusion scan, stress MRI). Invasive coronary angiography for high pretest
  probability or for revascularisation planning. **Exercise ECG** is not
  recommended for diagnosis in NICE guidance.

  - Assess risk: calcium score, ejection fraction, ischaemic burden.

treatment: >+
  - **GTN spray** for acute relief (teach the patient how to use it and what to
  do if pain persists beyond 5–10 minutes: call 999).

  - **Anti-anginal therapy:** first-line **β-blocker or rate-limiting
  calcium-channel blocker** (verapamil or diltiazem). If inadequate, use the
  other class (if combining with a β-blocker, use a dihydropyridine such as
  amlodipine). Second-line and add-ons: long-acting nitrates, ivabradine,
  nicorandil, ranolazine.

  - **Secondary prevention:** aspirin 75 mg (or clopidogrel), **high-intensity
  statin** (atorvastatin 80 mg, with ezetimibe as needed), ACE inhibitor or ARB
  (diabetes, hypertension, HF, CKD), BP and diabetes control, smoking cessation,
  weight loss, exercise, cardiac rehabilitation.

  - **Revascularisation:** PCI or CABG for symptoms despite optimal medical
  therapy, and CABG for left main or multivessel disease (especially with
  diabetes or poor LV function). Decision by the heart team.

protocols: >+
  - **Rest pain or unstable pattern:** treat as ACS (ECG, troponin, ACS
  pathway). **NICE CG126** (stable angina), **CG95** (chest pain of recent
  onset).

  - Do not give nitrates with recent PDE-5 inhibitors (sildenafil). Caution with
  β-blockers and rate-limiting CCBs together. Driving (DVLA) advice for angina.
